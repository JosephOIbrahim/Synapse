12Contested findings

Five of sixteen crucible findings are rejected in whole or in part. Two are factually wrong and verified so this pass. Three are right in substance and wrong in framing, scope, or sequencing. Nothing was dropped silently.

Thirteen findings were folded at source and are visible in §§1–11 under the revision key. What follows is everything I declined, with the reasoning and the anchor that settles it. Two of these are cases where the crucible mis-cited a line the draft had right — recorded not to score a point but because a builder navigating by these numbers would be sent to the wrong line, and because a report that silently accepts a wrong correction is no more trustworthy than one that silently ignores a right one.

12.3  partially rejected  "VERIFY is not half-honest; it is fully advisory"

The finding: apply-guarantees lens, severity 4 — the draft's "half of VERIFY is honest and half is silent" overstates the wire half, because wired: False changes nothing: status is still BUILT, ok() is True, errors stays empty, and no consumer of parameters_set or connections[*].wired exists tree-wide.

What I accept — all of the substance. Every one of those facts checks out, and the consequence is worse than the draft said: an apply where every connection failed to wire and every parm was unreadable still returns BUILT / ok()==True. That is now L3-7, and W6 grew a clause (b) specifically to fix it, because resolved: bool alone would have been a field nobody reads. The draft's S sizing was wrong and is now S–M.

What I reject — the collapse of two axes into one word. "Honest" and "consumed" are different properties, and the two failures need different fixes:

 Record accuracyConsumptionFix
observed: None on an unresolvable parmFalse — indistinguishable from a real null readnoneMake the record true (W6a) and give it a verdict (W6b)
wired: False on a failed connectionTrue — it accurately reports the live scenenoneGive it a verdict (W6b) only

Calling both "fully advisory" describes the shared symptom and hides that one of them is also lying. A builder who reads "fully advisory" fixes consumption and ships the false record intact. §6.4 therefore states both axes explicitly rather than adopting the finding's sentence, and §6.6 splits them into L3-3 (false record) and L3-7 (true record, no verdict).

12.4  partially rejected  "10s is reachable"

The finding: apply-guarantees lens, severity 5 — the highest-severity item in the whole crucible. An apply exceeding _DEFAULT_TIMEOUT = 10.0 reports FAILED while the build completes; the store does not consume; a retry double-builds.

What I accept — the entire defect, at full weight. It is corrected at source in §6.2(b), given its own diagram, promoted to W4 at the head of Band B, and named in §13 as the thing riskier than it looks. Every mechanical claim verified this pass: _DEFAULT_TIMEOUT = 10.0 at main_thread.py:21, started[0] = True before fn() at :564, and ProposalStore.get() returning entry[1] with no built-marker across all 34 lines of proposal_store.py.

What I reject — stating reachability as fact. The finding's evidence for "10s is reachable" is an enumeration of the work inside the payload, not a measurement. No apply has been timed. Writing "10s is reachable" into the report would put an unmeasured number behind a producer path that does not exist, which is the exact failure mode the control pinned to the brief's figure lesson is about: a figure copied from an argument, then cited as evidence for that argument.

Why the rejection costs nothing. The defect is structural. A bound that abandons without stopping, over a store that does not consume, is wrong at any frequency — a once-a-month silent double-build is arguably worse than a daily one, because nobody will ever correlate it. So W4 is scheduled on the defect and G5 measures the frequency separately, recorded as U9. The number sets W4's urgency, never its necessity.

12.5  horn declined  "Pull W12 (audience axis) ahead of W11"

The finding: buildability lens, severity 2 — W12 is the producer for two things ordered ahead of it. W9 and W10 ship a double approval on applies (§7.3); W11 bolts on a change boundary that W12 then re-expresses. Offered as an either/or: promote W12, or annotate the downstream rows.

What I accept — both consequences are real and neither was written down. W10 and W11 now carry explicit "Ships with" rows naming the double approval and the provisional boundary, so a builder who meets the second approval after W10 does not read it as a bug in W10 and try to fix it there. W11's row also states the composition constraint that keeps it ungated (§8.2).

What I decline — the promotion. §9 ranks on verification throughput gained × inverse cost, with Band B ranked on risk retired. W12 is L, gated, and gains no throughput; promoting it ahead of Band A's XS-to-S ungated work inverts the principle the whole section runs on, and it would put the one item that cannot start without an external authorization in front of eleven items that can start today.

What I adopt from the promotion horn instead: the finding's real insight is that W12's cost is authorization latency, not engineering. So — request the CTO authorization in parallel with Band A, and let it mature while W1–W8 run. W12 stays last in build order and stops being the thing Band D waits on. That is recorded in W12's Sequencing note.
