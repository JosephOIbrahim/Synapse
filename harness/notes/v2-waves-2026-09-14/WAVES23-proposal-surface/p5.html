09Build order

Re-ranked. The draft's #1 lost its leverage and gained three unenumerated consumers; the crucible added four items that were not on the board; one item split in two.

Corrected — why the order moved, in one paragraph

The draft ranked nine waves on a single axis: verification throughput gained × inverse cost. That axis still governs, but it cannot rank a correctness defect — an item that gains no throughput and retires a silent failure. So the order is now banded, and each band says which axis it is ranked on.

Three specific moves. Old W1 (propose_graph read-only) fell from #1 to W8: its headline unblock is refuted (§7.2) and its mechanism turns out to flip four behaviours, one of them harmful. Old W2 (catalog oracle) rose to W1: it survived the audit intact and grew in value when U5 closed. The apply-path findings became Band B, which did not exist in the draft.

Band A — verification throughput

Ranked on throughput gained × inverse cost. All ungated, all headless-testable, none larger than S–M. These are what stop the verification loop from needing a Houdini seat.

W8 propose_graph classification — a four-consumer decision was W1 — demoted

The question
Not "add it to the frozenset" but "what classification does a validate-and-park op actually want?" Membership in _READ_ONLY_COMMANDS buys lock-freedom and envelope-skip, and simultaneously costs Tier-0 provenance (core/floor_gate.py:292-306), resilience and latency tracking (websocket.py:686-693), and F3 freeze protection (hwebserver_adapter.py:236-257). The last is actively harmful: _handle_propose_graph is run_on_main-bound (handlers_graph_synth.py:69), so post-change a propose during a freeze stops fast-failing and starts queueing behind the frozen main thread.
Likely answer
A third classification — record-but-do-not-lock — rather than membership. Decide (a) whether propose keeps a Tier-0 record, (b) an explicit exclusion so it still hits the F3 breaker gate, or a plain statement that freeze-time proposes will hang.

Size
S–M (the draft said XS, 1 line + comment)
New test
Lock-freedom, plus an F3 regression test: with the transport breaker open, a propose must still fast-fail rather than queue. Plus a second test that holds the main thread (not the lock) and asserts what a propose actually does then — that is the honest measure of "verification throughput," and the draft's test could not see it.
Unblock — qualified
Proposals validate during background or farm renders that hold the C5 lock without occupying the main thread. Not during a foreground render — that blocks on the marshal either way (§7.2). Also retires L3-5's 65 false mutation records, at the cost of whatever record the decision chooses to keep.
