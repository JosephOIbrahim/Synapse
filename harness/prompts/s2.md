=== THE PROMPT ===

> It is Q2 2027. A mid-sized VFX studio — call it 40 artists, 3 Houdini
> generalists, one pipeline TD at 50% — ran SYNAPSE on real shots for one
> quarter. **They stopped using it.**
>
> Write the account of what happened.

**This is not pessimism. It is the only framing that produces checkable answers.** "What could go wrong" invites a list of generic risks. "It failed, write the account" forces a mechanism, a sequence, and a moment — and every one of those can be checked against the code as it stands today.

=== THE RULES THAT MAKE THIS WORTH READING ===

**Every failure mode names a mechanism that exists NOW.** Not "adoption might be slow" — that is a mood. "The PDG rollback raises TypeError on every invocation (`bridge.py:1718`, verified), so a failed TOPS cook leaves partial state and the TD who investigates finds SYNAPSE's own log claiming a rollback that never ran" is a failure mode. It has a file, a line, a person, and a consequence.

**Rank by LIKELIHOOD, not severity.** A catastrophic failure that requires six coincidences matters less than a mundane one that happens in week one. Studios do not drop tools because of the worst thing that could happen; they drop them because of the annoying thing that happens every Tuesday.

**Separate the three kinds, because they have different responses:**

```
FIXABLE     a defect. Name the fix and its rough cost.
LIMIT       true of the architecture. It gets DOCUMENTED, not fixed, and
            the honest question is whether a studio can live with it.
UNKNOWN     we cannot tell without a user. Name what observation would settle it.
```

**The UNKNOWN bucket is the most important one and it will be large.** SYNAPSE has ZERO production users. Anything about how an artist reacts, what they try first, or what they find confusing is UNKNOWN — and labelling it so is the single most useful thing this leg does, because it marks exactly where the next quarter's evidence has to come from.

=== THE QUESTIONS BEHIND THE STORY ===

**What breaks first?** Week one, not month three.

**What breaks WORST?** The incident that ends the trial in one afternoon rather than eroding it.

**What does the artist blame?** A tool that fails invisibly gets blamed for things it did not do. Three of the four install traps end with *no error, just absence* — what does that do to trust when something unrelated goes wrong?

**What does the pipeline TD say in the meeting?** They are the one who decides. Not the artist.

**And the one that decides everything: WHY DID THEY STOP?** Studios rarely drop a tool because it broke. They drop it because the value was never legible enough to justify the babysitting. Distinguish "it failed" from "it worked and nobody could tell."
