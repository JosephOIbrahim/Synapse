SYNAPSE

Synapse is a rope, not an elevator. It gives you reach you haven't earned yet and safety while you earn it — but you're still the one climbing.

Everything below assumes you know Houdini and don't know Synapse. If a section tells you something you already knew, skip it; nothing here builds on what came before.

Your first prompt

Open the Synapse pane, type what you want in plain English, press Enter. That's the whole interface.

Start with something small and verifiable, so you can see what it did:

make a grid, scatter 500 points on it, copy spheres to the points

You'll watch nodes appear in your network as it works. When it finishes, look at the network — not at the chat. The nodes are the deliverable; the conversation is just how you asked for them.

If nothing happens

Press Connect in the panel header. The bridge doesn't start by itself. If Connect is already showing Bridge ✓, the connection is fine and the problem is elsewhere — check the status line under the wordmark for what Houdini is reporting.

The three profiles

The tabs on the right — Curious, Expert, ML — change how much the panel explains and how densely it packs the screen. They do not change what Synapse can do. Every profile has identical capability; switching adds no tools and removes none.

Curious

For finding your footing. More air between things, the readouts you can't act on yet folded away, and the agent narrates each decision in plain language — errors translated, jargon defined the first time it appears.

Expert

The panel at full density. Every widget visible, no narration, no explanation you didn't ask for. More of the conversation fits on screen.

ML

Expert's density with the economics surfaced — token counts and model choice readable without a click, replies terse and technical.

Synapse never switches profiles for you. It may tell you a different pace exists; moving there is your call.

What it's good at

Procedural setup and scene assembly — building networks, wiring parameters, scattering, instancing, layout. Solaris and USD stage work. Reading your scene and answering questions about it. Turning a working network into a digital asset.

It runs inside Houdini's own Python, so it isn't guessing at your scene — it's reading it.

What it isn't

Synapse is early software with an agent inside it. Read this part properly — it's the part that saves you an afternoon.

Undo is scene-level

Save before a large request. Undo reverses changes to your scene, but anything that touched disk — files written, caches, renders — stays written. Treat a big prompt like you'd treat someone else's HDA: save first.

It can be confidently wrong

The agent will sometimes build something plausible that isn't what you asked for, and describe it as if it were. Look at the network, not the summary. The nodes are the truth.

Licence limits are real

Some render paths are unavailable on Indie and Apprentice licences. If a render silently does nothing, that's usually why, not Synapse.

It runs on your machine, for you

Synapse assumes a single user on localhost. It isn't hardened for a shared or networked seat, and access control is off in local mode.

When something goes wrong

Run synapse_doctor first. It checks the things that are usually broken — connection, licence, environment, model access — and tells you which one it is. Most sessions that feel broken are one line of its output.

If the doctor is clean and the behaviour is still wrong, that's worth reporting: open an issue and paste the doctor output with it.

The measure

Synapse is measured by what you can do without it. If it's building things you couldn't build alone and you're learning nothing, it's failing at its job — however impressive the output looks.

A rope is load-bearing. It is never self-propelling. The gradient runs one way: it builds while you watch, then builds with pauses so you can follow and object, then explains the move and you make it yourself. Explanation rises at your pace. Automation never does.
